# Small-Group Accountability Apps and Shared/Co-op Game Precedents

## How do StickK, Focusmate, and accountability-buddy apps structure small-group accountability? What mechanics exist (stakes, co-presence, feeds, nudges, group streaks)?

### Takeaway
Small-group/buddy accountability tools rely on four largely separable mechanics — financial/reputational stakes (StickK), scheduled synchronous co-presence (Focusmate), matched small private cohorts with lightweight check-ins (Supporti, Cohorty), and hard "group streak" chains where one person's miss resets everyone's counter (HabiStreak, UPROOF, TinyAct, RollCall) — but in almost none of these does an individual's action visibly change a **shared entity/world**; the shared thing is usually a number (a streak count) or a feed, not a state you'd call a "game."

### Cited Findings
- StickK: users create a "Commitment Contract" for a specific, measurable, time-bound goal and can put money ("stakes") on the line; failure forfeits the money to a friend, charity, or an "anti-charity" the user opposes; the card on file is charged within 48 hours of a failed report — [stickk.zendesk.com](https://stickk.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/206833157-How-it-Works), [stickk.com/faq/stakes](https://www.stickk.com/faq/stakes/Commitment+Contracts)
- Focusmate pairs a user with a partner (from the global community, not a chosen friend by default) for a scheduled synchronous video co-working session (25/50/75 min); both state their task goals at the start and report back at the end; camera-on visibility during the session and a "waiting partner" are the accountability mechanism, not a shared score or entity — [support.focusmate.com](https://support.focusmate.com/en/articles/9110188-getting-started), [blog.focusmate.com](https://blog.focusmate.com/finding-a-virtual-accountability-buddy-dos-and-donts/)
- Supporti is built around private groups of close friends/family/small teams plus one-to-one buddy matching and check-ins; there is no shared visual state — accountability is peer support/check-ins, not a mutual entity — [getsupporti.com](https://www.getsupporti.com/accountability-partner)
- Cohorty matches users into "cohorts" of 3–10 people (mixing invited friends and strangers to fill the cohort) doing the *same* challenge starting on the *same* date; interaction is capped at heart-reactions on daily check-ins ("no comments, no chat, no pressure"); cited research says accountability groups of 6–10 stayed active (80%+ regular check-ins) for 9.7 months on average vs. 4.2 months for 1:1 partnerships — [blog.cohorty.app](https://blog.cohorty.app/small-group-accountability-apps-complete-guide/)
- Coach.me's core accountability mechanic is a paid 1:1 human coach checking in via chat; it does not appear to offer a dedicated group-accountability feature the way a separate product, CoachAccountable, does (CoachAccountable offers "Group assignments" and "Group Metrics" so everyone can see the whole group's aggregate performance, but this is a coaching-business tool, not a consumer habit app) — [blog.coach.me](https://blog.coach.me/accountability-coaching/), [coachaccountable.com/groups](https://www.coachaccountable.com/groups)
- Group-streak apps that explicitly break a *shared* streak counter on any one member's miss: HabiStreak ("everyone must complete their daily task to keep the group's streak going, and one miss resets it"); UPROOF (entire group's streak resets from one miss, timestamped photo proof, escalating fines for missed deadlines); TinyAct (group accountability mode — any single member breaking the chain zeroes the whole group's streak); RollCall (daily photo-proof challenge for the whole group; streak lives until someone slips); Zenith ("independent shared mode" — individual quotas, but one failure zeroes the group streak) — [play.google.com HabiStreak](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kishorek.habistreak), app-store listings for UPROOF, TinyAct, RollCall, Zenith (via search aggregation)
- Way of Life uses individual (not group) "Chains" — customizable-length positive streaks — and "red chains" that track consecutive failures as a separate visual counter; no buddy/group mechanic was found tied to this feature in available sources — [Way of Life app listings](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/way-of-life-habit-tracker/id393159800)

### Inferences
- The market cluster of "group streak" apps (HabiStreak, UPROOF, TinyAct, RollCall, Zenith) is the closest existing precedent to "one shared counter driven by several people's real actions," but the shared state in all of them is abstract (a number/chain), not a visualized world, boss, or character — this looks like a genuine gap that a shared *game* state (vs. shared *streak number*) could fill.
- Stakes (StickK) and co-presence (Focusmate) are mechanically orthogonal to "shared state" — they increase the cost of failure or the social presence of accountability, but don't require or produce a shared visible artifact. A product could combine financial/social stakes AND a shared game entity, which none of the reviewed apps do simultaneously.
- Cohorty's cited retention stat (9.7 vs 4.2 months) suggests small-group (not just 1:1) social structures may have superior long-run adherence — relevant if pitching a "small group of 2-4 friends" instead of a solo experience.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Cohorty's 9.7-vs-4.2-month retention claim against an original/independent study — it appears only on Cohorty's own blog, which is a self-interested source; treat as marketing-supported claim, not peer-reviewed fact.
- No detail found on whether StickK supports a *shared* stake pool among a group (e.g., all 3 friends' money on the line for one shared outcome) vs. only individual contracts with a designated recipient.
- Could not find substantive independent (non-vendor) reviews evaluating whether these group-streak apps' shared-streak mechanic actually improves adherence versus individual streaks — most sourcing here is app-store/vendor copy.

## Habitica's Party and Guild system in depth: how does task completion by multiple real users affect a SHARED entity (boss fight, party health)?

### Takeaway
Habitica's Party Boss Quest is the single clearest existing precedent for "a shared game entity whose state is driven by multiple real people's individual real-world task completion": positive task completion by any party member chips a shared boss health bar, while any member's *missed* Daily deals shared damage to the *entire party's* HP pool (including members who did nothing wrong), and this is explicitly scoped to small, often real-life-friend parties rather than the whole public userbase.

### Cited Findings
- A Party is a group of Habitica players (any size, including a party of one) who quest together; parties can be "small groups of real-life friends or large groups of strangers," created via invite by @username or email (explicitly supporting inviting real-life friends), with a party leader who has administrative control — [search-derived from habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Party](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Party)
- Parties are capped at 30 members — [GitHub HabitRPG/habitica issue #8561](https://github.com/HabitRPG/habitica/issues/8561)
- Quests are initiated with a quest scroll sent to the party; the quest begins once all members respond, or the quest owner/party leader force-starts it — [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests) (via search)
- In a Boss Quest, players reduce the boss's shared health by completing Dailies, positive Habits, and To-Dos; positive clicks apply damage to the boss at the day's Cron (daily reset) — [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests), [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Boss](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Boss) (via search)
- Critically, during a Boss Quest, missing a Daily damages **both the individual and the rest of their party** — "if you miss a daily, not only do you get hurt, but the rest of your party also takes damage from the boss" — and even a party member who is "resting in the Inn" (a status that normally exempts a player from personal damage) still takes damage caused by *other* party members' missed Dailies — [search-derived, habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Boss](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Boss), [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Mechanics](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Mechanics)
- Damage scales with boss strength and the difficulty/number of missed Dailies: stronger bosses multiply missed-Daily damage several-fold, and Dailies marked "Hard" or already turned red (missed multiple times) deal more damage than "Easy" or blue (fresh) Dailies — [search-derived, habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Damage_to_Player](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Damage_to_Player)
- Quests give "the added incentive of accountability to complete Dailies/Habits/To-Dos" specifically because failure is shared — [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests) (via search)
- Habitica *also* runs "World Boss" events, which are explicitly the large-scale/public counterpart to a party boss quest: **all** Habitica users are automatically enrolled (even if already in a party quest), participation is free, and critically — unlike the small-party version — "players will not take damage from a world boss attack or have their accounts negatively affected in any way." This is a deliberate design split: shared *upside* (rewards for everyone) at public scale, but shared *downside* (mutual damage from individual failure) reserved for the small, opt-in party — [search-derived, habitica.fandom.com/wiki/World_Bosses](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/World_Bosses)
- Guilds (the larger, topic/interest-based public or private communities, discontinued August 8, 2023) explicitly **could not host quests** — "if you want to fight monsters with other people, you must be in the same party," reinforcing that the shared-boss/shared-damage mechanic was structurally reserved for the small Party unit, not the larger Guild community — [search-derived, habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Guilds](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Guilds), [habitica.com/static/faq/tavern-and-guilds](https://habitica.com/static/faq/tavern-and-guilds)
- Only players who joined a quest before it started can contribute to/benefit from it; joining a party mid-quest means waiting for the current quest to finish before participating in the next one — [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests) (via search)

### Inferences
- Habitica's own design choice — mutual damage/shared risk ONLY at small-party scale, reward-only/no-risk at public "World Boss" scale — is strong existing-product evidence that shared *downside* risk mechanics (one person's failure hurting everyone) are viewed by an experienced live-ops team as appropriate for small trusted groups but not for large/public participation, which validates a "2-4 friends, real skin in the game" design over a leaderboard-of-strangers design.
- The party-of-30 cap (vs. unlimited guilds) further shows Habitica treats "party" as structurally a small/medium trusted unit — though 30 is still much larger than the 2-4 friend group in question; there's no evidence Habitica ever offered a smaller hard cap (e.g., 4) as its own distinct tier.
- The "Hard/red Dailies deal more damage" rule suggests a design lesson: escalating shared consequences for repeated individual failure (not just flat shared damage) may keep the shared-entity mechanic feeling fair over time.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the primary Habitica Fandom wiki pages directly (WebFetch returned HTTP 402 Payment Required on habitica.fandom.com), so all Habitica mechanic details above are reconstructed from Google-indexed search snippets of those wiki pages rather than the full primary text — treat exact numeric damage formulas as approximate/paraphrased, not verbatim-quoted.
- Could not find official Habitica documentation on what happens when the *shared party* HP (as opposed to an individual's HP) hits zero during a boss quest — i.e., is there a "party wipe" state distinct from individual character death? Sources describe individual damage-taking from party mechanics but not a distinct "shared HP pool" separate from each character's own health bar. This is an important mechanical nuance (shared boss HP bar vs. each player's own HP being damaged by others' failures) that could not be fully disambiguated from available snippets.
- No first-hand r/habitrpg user-experience threads were successfully retrieved (search returned only tangential/unrelated results); real-user qualitative accounts of how party quests feel socially (resentment when a slacking friend hurts the group, etc.) are missing and would strengthen the case for/against this mechanic.

## Other examples of a shared/co-op game state (boss, build, garden, world) changing based on multiple people's individual real-world task completion — including fitness apps and non-habit games

### Takeaway
Strava's "Group Goal" challenge type and Habitica boss quests are the two clearest verified precedents for a shared cumulative target driven by multiple real people's real activity; Peloton's "Teams" and "high fives" are social/competitive layers on top of *individual* leaderboards rather than a single mutated shared entity; several small "habit garden" apps exist but genuinely shared (multi-person) gardens are a niche/emerging pattern with thin, mostly-unverified precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Strava Group Challenges include a "Group Goal" type: participants "chase a goal as a group" (a time, distance, elevation gain, or elevation loss target), all members' activities count toward one single shared goal, and — notably — this type has **no leaderboard**, only a view of "how you're contributing to the group effort" — [support.strava.com/Group Challenges](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401736-how-do-group-challenges-work-on-strava)
- Other Strava Group Challenge types (Most Activity, Fastest Effort, Longest Single Activity) are comparative/leaderboard-based rather than a shared cumulative entity — [support.strava.com](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401736-how-do-group-challenges-work-on-strava)
- A challenge creator can invite up to 199 followers, and participants can be allowed to invite their own followers too — group challenges' leaderboards are private to participants (vs. public leaderboards on open Strava Challenges) — [support.strava.com](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401736-how-do-group-challenges-work-on-strava)
- Peloton "Teams" let users build invite-only or public groups of up to 50,000 to "work towards shared goals, compete against each other, or send encouraging messages," and a "Here Now" leaderboard view shows members taking the same live/on-demand class simultaneously — [onepeloton.com/blog](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-community-features)
- Peloton's "high five" is an ephemeral per-class social nudge (throttled to one per minute per recipient) that disappears after class ends with no persistent record — it is a social gesture, not a mechanic that changes a shared game state — [support.onepeloton.com](https://support.onepeloton.com/s/article/360046166092-Sending-A-High-Five)
- A shared weekly Peloton Team leaderboard aggregates total time/workouts per team, which is a shared *statistic* but not a shared visualized world/boss/character — [onepeloton.com/blog](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-community-features)
- Habit-garden apps exist where an individual's own habit completion grows/wilts a personal virtual garden (Pixelgarden, Habits Garden, "Digital Habit Garden" on GitHub); Pixelgarden adds a *minor* social feature — a friend can "water your wilting plants once a day" via a shared link — but the garden itself remains individually owned — [search-derived, app listings for Pixelgarden, Habits Garden, github.com/Amr-Sadek/digital-habit-garden]
- A GitHub project, "shared-garden" by user C0derTang, describes itself explicitly as "a shared garden for two people, grown through small daily acts of care," where both members see permanent flowers grow in expanding pixel beds based on both people's real daily contributions, with a shared streak and read-only history — [github.com/C0derTang/shared-garden](https://github.com/C0derTang/shared-garden)
- Idle/MMO games with a shared-boss precedent for the game-mechanic pattern itself (not tied to real-world habits): Realm Idle's weekly World Boss where "every player's damage feeds one shared hunt" and milestone rewards pay everyone who fought, plus a shared community funding pool that "buffs everyone at once" once a tier is reached; IdleMMO's World Bosses are a "shared adversary" players collaboratively vanquish — [search-derived, wiki.idle-mmo.com/combat/world-bosses, Realm Idle app description]
- MagicTask, a task-gamification app, explicitly ties a "boss fight" cinematic reward to completing a real task ("you finish the work, you get the boss fight") but this appears to be an individual, not shared/multi-person, mechanic based on available description — [magictask.io](https://www.magictask.io/)

### Inferences
- Strava's deliberate choice to drop the leaderboard specifically for the "shared cumulative goal" challenge type (vs. keeping it for comparative types) suggests a recognized design principle: when the point is a *shared* target, ranking individuals against each other undercuts the cooperative framing — relevant if designing a co-op game state where friends shouldn't feel ranked against each other.
- The C0derTang shared-garden project is a low-visibility, apparently very recently built (open PR history, no press coverage found) two-person project — it is a real, functioning precedent for the *exact* mechanic in question (individual real actions from 2 people building one shared visual world) but is not an established/market-validated product; it should be cited as "a small existing example this exact idea has been built before, at tiny scale," not as proof of market fit.
- No large fitness or game company appears to have shipped a persistent, visually-mutating shared "world/character" (as opposed to a shared number/goal-bar or a shared leaderboard) driven by a *small private* group's real-world task completion — this remains a comparatively open niche.

### Gaps
- Could not independently verify the current operating status, user base, or reception of the C0derTang shared-garden project — it reads as a small/personal or recently AI-assisted build (PR titles read like automated commit summaries) rather than a widely used app; treat with caution and do not present as validated market precedent.
- Could not find a large-scale (>2 person, "real" market) shared-visual-world app beyond the idle-MMO precedents that are pure games (no real-world habit tie-in) and the tiny two-person garden project that does the opposite (real habit tie-in, but no scale). The gap between "large-scale co-op game state" and "real-habit-driven small-group state" is not clearly bridged by any single existing product found in this research.
- Did not find documentation on Peloton "Teams" having any mechanic where an individual's ride specifically mutates a single shared visual/game object (as opposed to aggregating into a shared statistic) — this distinction (shared number vs. shared world/entity) could not be fully confirmed either way for Peloton Teams beyond the leaderboard framing.

## Is there precedent for a small private group where each person's individual daily performance visibly and directly affects a shared resource/world/character everyone can see (vs. parallel individual leaderboards)?

### Takeaway
The clearest verified precedent remains Habitica's Party Boss Quest (shared boss HP driven by, and shared damage inflicted on, the whole small private party) plus Strava's private "Group Goal" challenges (shared cumulative bar, no individual ranking); most other small-group products (Cohorty, Supporti, group-streak apps) share a *streak count* or *check-in feed*, not a mutable world/character, and true "everyone sees one shared world/character move because of what a small trusted group did" precedent outside Habitica is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Habitica Party Boss Quests: see full detail above — a private/invite-based, real-life-friend-eligible party (up to 30, but usable at 2-4) collectively damages one shared boss and collectively suffers party-wide damage from missed Dailies, and this content is only visible to fellow quest/party members, not the public — [habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests, Boss, Party](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Quests) (via search)
- Strava Group Goal challenges: a private (participant-only) cumulative bar filled by all members' real activity, explicitly designed without a leaderboard so the group sees only collective progress, not rank — [support.strava.com](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401736-how-do-group-challenges-work-on-strava)
- Group-streak apps (HabiStreak, UPROOF, TinyAct, RollCall, Zenith): the shared "resource" is a single streak counter that any member's daily performance can zero out for the whole group, and it is visible to the whole (small, private-by-nature-of-invite) group — but it is a number, not a world/character with visual state — [play.google.com and app-store listings, cited above]
- Cohorty and Supporti explicitly frame themselves around small private groups (3-10 people; close friends/family/tight-knit teams) with visible cross-member progress, but the "shared" element is a feed of individual check-ins/hearts, not a single mutated entity — [blog.cohorty.app](https://blog.cohorty.app/small-group-accountability-apps-complete-guide/), [getsupporti.com](https://www.getsupporti.com/accountability-partner)
- The C0derTang two-person shared-garden project (see above) is a small, low-visibility but directly on-point example of exactly this pattern at the smallest possible scale (n=2) — [github.com/C0derTang/shared-garden](https://github.com/C0derTang/shared-garden)

### Inferences
- Habitica's party mechanic is the strongest, most-established precedent that exists for the exact pattern the key question asks about; everything else found is either (a) a shared *number* rather than a shared *visualized entity* (group streaks, Strava group goal, Peloton team stats), or (b) a shared visualized entity but at unverified/tiny scale (shared-garden GitHub project).
- This supports treating "small private group + one shared game/world/character visibly mutated by everyone's real daily performance" as a genuinely differentiated design space: the nearest big, proven precedent (Habitica) buries this mechanic inside a much larger, general-purpose RPG task manager rather than making it the core product, and the mechanic there is 12+ years old with no evidence of major recent competitors replicating just that piece as a standalone product.

### Gaps
- No independent, large-scale consumer product was found whose *entire* premise (not just one feature among many) is "small private group, shared game world, driven by individual real habits" — this may genuinely be a validated gap, or research may simply have missed a niche/regional competitor; further targeted search for very recent (2024-2026) app-store launches specifically pitching "co-op habit RPG" or "habit game with friends" would be needed to fully close this gap.

## Zombies Run! and small-group versions of narrative-driven real-world fitness games

### Takeaway
Zombies, Run! is a solo narrative-audio running game (real running drives a fictional zombie-outbreak story), and per its own developers it was explicitly *not* designed for synchronous multiplayer; social features added later (friend feed, virtual races) are asynchronous and competitive/comparative rather than a shared co-op world state, and the developers have only ever discussed (not shipped, per available sources) a friend-co-op "shared base" concept.

### Cited Findings
- Zombies, Run! casts the player as "Runner Five," playing story audio clips interspersed with the runner's own music as they run in real life; missions ask the player to "collect supplies and intelligence" for a survivor community — [tomchatfield.net](https://tomchatfield.net/portfolio/the-story-behind-zombies-run/), [alphr.com](https://www.alphr.com/business/1005907/getting-fit-with-the-undead-the-zombies-run-story/)
- Writer Naomi Alderman and CEO Adrian Hon designed the zombie-chase mechanic specifically because it maps cleanly onto running motivation: "zombies can't be bargained with — they just keep on coming, and that makes them good motivation to keep on running" — [denofgeek.com interview](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/naomi-alderman-interview-zombies-run-crying-and-music/)
- The game's own support documentation states the experience "isn't designed for multiplayer"; the suggested workaround for running with a friend is manually syncing identical playlists on two devices with chases turned off — [support.zombiesrungame.com](https://support.zombiesrungame.com/hc/en-us/articles/203780666-Can-I-work-out-with-a-friend)
- A 2016 feature, "ZL Social & Friend Feed," added the ability to add friends and view their public runs in a feed — an asynchronous, comparative social layer, not a shared game state — [blog.zombiesrungame.com](https://blog.zombiesrungame.com/2016/07/13/introducing-zl-social-friend-feed/)
- Zombies, Run! virtual races give a medal/bib/certificate/leaderboard placement for a run completed anytime within a week-long window — asynchronous and comparative, not co-op — [search-derived, zombiesrungame virtual races]
- A 2015 fan blog comment/dev response indicates users have directly requested (and the developers have discussed, without confirming a ship date) two co-op ideas: (1) "a base you and your friend can collect for together" (i.e., exactly a shared, jointly-built resource) and (2) synchronous shared-story group running; developers stated they see asynchronous co-op as more feasible than synchronous co-op, since most players run solo — [blog.zombiesrungame.com](https://blog.zombiesrungame.com/2015/06/24/hey-guys-just-wanted-to-let-you-know-how-much-im/)

### Inferences
- Even the most famous real-world-fitness-driven narrative game deliberately avoided building the "shared base/world driven by a small group's combined real running" feature that users explicitly asked for — the developers' own stated reasoning (most runners run alone; synchronous multiplayer is technically/socially harder than asynchronous) suggests real friction reasons *why* this pattern is rare, not just an overlooked opportunity. Any product attempting the shared-world-for-friends pattern should expect to solve this same coordination problem (people completing habits at different times/paces) rather than assuming it's simply unexplored.
- Zombies, Run!'s core motivational insight (concrete, personified antagonist tied directly and only to real physical action) is a reusable design lesson independent of the multiplayer question — it argues for a legible, single-minded "threat" or "goal" object (like a boss) as more motivating than an abstract stat.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the "shared base" co-op feature was ever actually shipped by Zombies, Run! / Six to Start at any point after 2015 — available sources only show the original feature request and a developer response expressing interest, with no follow-up confirming or denying a later release. This should be flagged as an open question, not assumed to be either shipped or abandoned.
- No other narrative real-world-fitness game (competitor to Zombies, Run!) was researched in this pass; the assignment's suggested scope was specifically Zombies, Run!, so competitors (e.g., The Walk, Superhero Workout) were not investigated and remain a gap if needed for a fuller landscape.
