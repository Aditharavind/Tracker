# 75 Hard App Market & Community Research

## What dedicated "75 Hard" tracker apps exist, and what is their UI paradigm?

### Takeaway
The 75 Hard tracker category is crowded but homogeneous: dozens of iOS/Android apps (plus Notion/Gumroad templates) all converge on the same UI paradigm — a daily checklist of the 5 non-negotiable tasks, a streak/progress counter, and a photo-journal timeline for progress pics. Ratings cluster high (4.2–4.9 stars) but review counts vary hugely (from dozens to ~8,700), and no dedicated 75 Hard app uses a genuinely novel UI paradigm beyond checklist + calendar + photos.

### Cited Findings
- The official app, simply "75 Hard," published by 44SEVEN MEDIA LLC (Andy Frisella's company), costs $4.99, and offers a daily task checklist, custom reminders per task, in-app progress-photo capture/review, one-tap sharing to Instagram, personal notes per day, and history across multiple attempts (including continuing into the year-long "Live Hard" program) — [andyfrisella.com](https://andyfrisella.com/products/75-hard-app); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/75-hard/id1502228408)
- App Store search reports the official 75 Hard app rated 4.7/5 from 207 reviews in one snapshot and 4.55/5 from 6,406 reviews / 3.55 out of 5 from 790 ratings on Google Play in other snapshots — figures vary by source/date and platform, indicating rating volatility or differing count methodologies — [search summary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/75-hard/id1502228408); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.media44seven.seventyfivehard&hl=en_US)
- "BeHard" (75 Days Challenge Tough & Soft) is one of the highest-rated apps in the category at 4.78–4.82 stars across roughly 8,700–13,900 App Store ratings; it supports 5 challenge templates, an AI coach, custom challenges, water tracking, progress photos, a widget, and "friend invites with shared progress" — [TapSmart review](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/75-hard-review/); [Reset75 comparison](https://reset75.com/compare/best-75-hard-apps/)
- "Reset75" offers 6 input types (checkbox, counter, duration, photo, text, measurement), timelapse videos of progress photos, a statistics dashboard, 39 languages, offline mode, and widgets; rated 4.86★ (78 App Store reviews) / 4.85★ (430+ Google Play reviews) — [reset75.com comparison](https://reset75.com/compare/best-75-hard-apps/)
- "Tough Habits" offers a daily checklist, up to 3 custom goals, and progress photos, rated 4.77★ from 2,474 reviews; "Solowei" offers task checklist, water tracking, weight logging, progress photos, and a "community feature," rated 4.23★ from 3,730 reviews — [reset75.com comparison](https://reset75.com/compare/best-75-hard-apps/)
- Other named entrants found via App Store/Google Play search: "75 Hard: Challenge Tracker" (Shay Alon Vash, free w/ IAP, supports 75/28/100-day variants), "75 Hard Challenge: Day Tracker" (Aric Carmel), "Tracker For 75 Days Hard" (Nesredin Ahmed, free w/ IAP, charts/graphs + photo journal), "Habits & 75 Hard Tracker" (Soumaneel Konar, presets for 75 Hard/Monk Mode/Cold Shower/No Zero Days), "75 Soft Challenge Tracker," "75 Days Challenge Tough & Soft," "Transform 75," "Forge 75 Hard & Soft Challenge," "75Me: 75 Day Hard Life-Reset" — [App Store search results](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/75-hard-challenge-tracker/id6752606716)
- Non-app tracking is also heavily served by paid Notion templates and Gumroad spreadsheets (typically $1–5): e.g. a free Notion "75 Hard Challenge Tracker" template downloaded over 15,000 times, plus numerous Google Sheets templates with workout/meal planners — [Notion Marketplace](https://www.notion.com/templates/75-hard-challenge-tracker-liyahgwood); [Harrison Wallace blog](https://harrisonwallace.co/blog/free-75-hard-challenge-tracker-notion-template)
- Common reported technical issues across apps: unreliable first-time photo uploads requiring app restart, one app not updated since September 2023, unreliable photo cropping, missing water tracking/widgets/custom challenge lengths, and repeated forced re-login prompts — [WebSearch summary of App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/75-days-challenge-tough-soft/id6450723685?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)

### Inferences
- The category is saturated with near-identical checklist/photo-journal apps differentiated mainly by minor feature additions (AI coach, widgets, multi-language) and by challenge-length flexibility (75 Hard/Soft/28/100-day variants) rather than by UI innovation — this suggests low differentiation and a real opening for a fundamentally different interaction paradigm.
- Rating and review-count figures conflict across search snapshots for the same app (e.g., official 75 Hard app rating cited as both 4.7/207 and 4.55/6,406 and 3.55/790), likely reflecting different dates, platforms (iOS vs Android), or aggregator methodology — treat exact numbers as directional, not precise.

### Gaps
- Could not independently verify exact, current App Store/Google Play star ratings and review counts via primary store pages (searches returned aggregator/AI-summarized figures rather than confirmed live listing data); a developer should pull live figures directly from App Store Connect/Google Play Console competitor research or manually visit each listing.
- No confirmed total download counts were found for most apps (only "75 Hard Together" had an explicit download figure: ~1,500 installs since September 2024, per AppBrain-style aggregation).

## Does any existing 75 Hard app use game-like mechanics (avatars, levels, XP, virtual world, platformer/side-scroller, pets/companions)? Does a Mario-style platformer 75 Hard app exist?

### Takeaway
No dedicated 75 Hard app was found that uses avatars, XP/leveling, a virtual world, pets, or — specifically — a Mario-style/side-scrolling platformer UI. Gamification (XP, levels, RPG classes) exists only in a small set of *generic* habit-tracker/RPG apps that mention 75 Hard as one of several "programs" or inspirations, not as a dedicated game skin; this looks like a clear, confirmed white space.

### Cited Findings
- A direct comparison of 6 leading 75 Hard-specific apps (Reset75, official 75 Hard, BeHard, Solowei, Tough Habits, Her 75) found: "None of the apps employ gamification mechanics (avatars, XP, levels, pets). All emphasize accountability through visual progress tracking or social features rather than game-like rewards." — [Reset75 comparison page](https://reset75.com/compare/best-75-hard-apps/) (synthesized via WebFetch)
- Searches for "75 hard" + "Mario," "platformer," "side-scroller," or "pixel art" returned no matching app; results were entirely unrelated (Mario game journalism, generic itch.io pixel-art asset packs, an unrelated Mario Royale battle-royale fangame that only coincidentally involves the number "75" as a player count) — [WebSearch: 75 hard app game mario platformer]; [WebSearch: pixel art side-scroller 75 hard]
- "Disciplinex: Level up in IRL" is a generic gamified discipline app that references 75 Hard/75 Soft as one of the frameworks it draws on, and tracks execution rate, streaks, and XP as you "level up" — but it is not a 75-Hard-specific skin, let alone a platformer — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/disciplinex-level-up-in-irl/id6743348577)
- Other RPG-style habit apps surfaced in search (Habitica, HeroFit, LevelsUp, Solo Leveling Daily Quests, Solo Leveling Your Self, Quest Tasks, Gamify: Turn Life into a Game, Level Up – Gamified Fitness, Skillion) are all general-purpose habit/fitness gamification apps with avatars, XP, RPG classes, or quest framing — none are built around or marketed specifically as 75 Hard trackers, and none described use a horizontally-scrolling platformer interface; they use RPG-menu, character-sheet, or quest-list UIs instead — [WebSearch: 75 hard gamified XP levels avatar app]; [WebSearch: 75 hard RPG quest level up game app discipline]

### Inferences
- Because gamified habit apps (Habitica-style) exist broadly but no one has applied that gamification specifically and thoroughly to 75 Hard, and because no platformer/side-scrolling UI exists in this niche at all, a Mario-style platformer skin for 75 Hard would currently be a novel, uncontested positioning in the market as of this research (September 2026).
- The absence of gamification in dedicated 75 Hard apps may reflect the program's own "brutal/no-excuses" branding (Andy Frisella's ethos is explicitly anti-soft, anti-reward-culture), which could mean either (a) an opportunity, since no one has captured a lighter/game-loving segment, or (b) a risk, since some fraction of the core audience may see game mechanics as clashing with the program's intentionally punishing identity. This is inference, not sourced fact.

### Gaps
- Coverage is limited to what standard web search surfaces; a small/unlisted or very recently launched app (Product Hunt "maker" launch, TikTok-only marketing, or a game published under a non-"75 hard" name) could exist without appearing in these queries. Confidence is high but not absolute — recommend a targeted App Store/Google Play in-store search for "platformer," "pixel," "RPG," or "game" filtered to health/fitness category by a human reviewer as a final check.

## How do people track 75 Hard when they DON'T use a dedicated app?

### Takeaway
Non-app tracking is dominated by three approaches: (1) paid Notion/Google Sheets templates sold on Gumroad and the Notion marketplace (typically $1–5, some free with 15,000+ downloads), (2) informal methods — plain notes apps, generic spreadsheets, or Instagram Stories used as a public commitment device, and (3) community/social accountability via Reddit (r/75Hard), Discord servers, and group chats. Marketing copy from at least one competing app explicitly frames these informal methods as inadequate ("none of which actually track consistency over 75 days").

### Cited Findings
- A free Notion "75 Hard Challenge Tracker" template has been downloaded over 15,000 times — [Harrison Wallace blog](https://harrisonwallace.co/blog/free-75-hard-challenge-tracker-notion-template)
- Numerous paid spreadsheet/Notion templates are sold via Gumroad and Notion Marketplace, most in the $1–5 range, including named products like "75 Hard | Progress Tracker," "Tracker for 75 HARD," and "75Hard tracker BW," typically bundling daily checklists for water/workouts/diet/reading/photos plus weight/calorie logs — [Gumroad search results]; [Notion Marketplace listings]
- An article aimed at people doing 75 Hard with friends states plainly that, absent a dedicated tool, "most people currently use informal methods: Notes, spreadsheets, or Instagram stories — none of which actually track consistency over 75 days" — [Daily Pact blog: 75 Hard With Friends](https://daily-pact.com/blog/75-hard-with-friends)
- The same article frames solo/informal tracking's core failure mode as invisibility: "Nobody notices when you're struggling on day 23," friends "can't verify if you actually completed workouts," and people experience "silent restarts" — abandoning the challenge without anyone knowing — [Daily Pact blog: 75 Hard With Friends](https://daily-pact.com/blog/75-hard-with-friends)
- A TikTok discovery page topic exists specifically titled "75 Hard Habit Tracker on Your Notes App with Friends," indicating a recognizable trend of people manually replicating shared tracking using their phone's default Notes app rather than a dedicated tool — [TikTok discover page](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/75-hard-habit-tracker-on-your-notes-app-with-friends)
- An accountability-focused article recommends people without a partner "stack" three informal/community accountability methods: community groups (r/75HARD subreddits and Discord servers), public commitment across social platforms, and app-based tracking — [Reset75 blog: 75 Hard Accountability Partner](https://reset75.com/blog/75-hard-accountability-partner/)

### Inferences
- Both competitor marketing pages (Daily Pact and Reset75) independently and explicitly frame "spreadsheets/notes/Instagram" as the default fallback and as insufficient — since this framing is self-serving (used to sell their own app), it should be read as evidence that a real gap exists in casual/manual tracking, but the specific characterization of *why* it fails is marketing language, not independently verified user research.
- The TikTok "Notes app with friends" trend suggests real organic demand for *shared* tracking that no tool has fully satisfied — people are hacking together a shared-checklist experience with a tool (Notes) not designed for it, which is a reasonably strong signal of unmet need for lightweight group tracking.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access primary Reddit thread content (reddit.com/r/75Hard) — WebFetch to reddit.com was blocked/unreachable in this environment, and web search results for site:reddit.com queries returned App Store listings rather than actual Reddit posts/comments. All community-behavior claims above come from secondary sources (competitor blogs referencing informal methods and Discord/Reddit as accountability venues) rather than primary Reddit quotes. A researcher with direct Reddit/Discord access should verify this section with primary threads.
- No hard data found on relative popularity of spreadsheet vs. Notion vs. Notes-app tracking (e.g., no download/usage counts comparing these methods to each other).

## What do r/75Hard and 75 Hard Facebook/Discord communities say about tracking pain points, accountability partners, and wishlist features?

### Takeaway
Primary-source Reddit/Facebook/Discord content could not be directly retrieved in this research session (Reddit fetches were blocked and searches surfaced app-store pages instead of threads); findings here are drawn from secondary sources (competitor blog posts that summarize or reference community sentiment) and should be treated as lower-confidence pending direct community access.

### Cited Findings
- Reset75's accountability guide states the core psychological pain point of the program is the "asymmetric restart penalty": missing even one task on any day resets the full 75-day count, meaning "one slip resets two months of work" — a much higher-stakes failure mode than typical habit trackers, which the guide argues is why solo tracking feels "psychologically challenging" — [Reset75 blog: 75 Hard Accountability Partner](https://reset75.com/blog/75-hard-accountability-partner/)
- The same guide cites external research (unspecified original study) claiming a group that wrote down goals, listed action commitments, and sent weekly progress updates to a friend completed 76% of their goals, versus 43% for a group that only thought about their goals — a 33-point gap attributed to external accountability — [Reset75 blog](https://reset75.com/blog/75-hard-accountability-partner/)
- Reset75 explicitly recommends r/75HARD and Discord servers as one of three "stacked" accountability methods for people without a personal accountability partner, alongside public social-media commitment and app-based tracking — [Reset75 blog](https://reset75.com/blog/75-hard-accountability-partner/)
- Daily Pact's blog frames group accountability mechanics people value as: social pressure (embarrassment at admitting a missed day), shared-experience bonding ("Day 14 in the rain," "Day 30 with a cold"), competition via a visible leaderboard, and peer rule-enforcement (friends catching rationalizations/gray-area cheating) — [Daily Pact blog](https://daily-pact.com/blog/75-hard-with-friends)

### Inferences
- Both cited sources agree independently that the single biggest tracking/accountability pain point specific to 75 Hard (vs. generic habit trackers) is the all-or-nothing restart rule, which makes missed-day *visibility* and social pressure disproportionately valuable compared to lower-stakes habit challenges. This is a reasonably well-triangulated point even though both sources are marketing content.
- The repeated emphasis (across independent competitor blogs) on leaderboards, shared visibility, and peer rule-enforcement suggests that any group/shared 75 Hard product's core value proposition should center on real-time visibility of others' status rather than just parallel individual tracking.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes, post titles, upvote counts, or comment threads from r/75Hard, a 75 Hard Facebook group, or a 75 Hard Discord were retrievable in this session — WebFetch to reddit.com returned "unable to fetch," and WebSearch queries targeting site:reddit.com consistently returned App Store/Google Play listings instead of actual Reddit content (likely because these app names/keywords heavily SEO-dominate search results). This is a meaningful gap: a follow-up researcher with browser/Reddit-API access should pull specific r/75Hard threads (e.g., search "tracker," "app," "accountability partner," "wish there was") to get primary-source pain points and exact community sentiment/quotes.
- No data found on Facebook group size/activity or Discord server membership numbers for 75 Hard communities.

## Is there market demand for group/shared 75 Hard tracking (vs. solo tracking)? What evidence exists of demand or existing shared/social features?

### Takeaway
Yes — there is clear, multi-source evidence of demand for group/shared 75 Hard tracking, and several apps already ship real-time friend/partner visibility features (not just generic "invite a friend" buttons), indicating this is a validated but not yet saturated sub-niche. A developer building a group feature would be entering a space with existing competitors, but the core "see friends' status in real time" mechanic is validated demand, not speculative.

### Cited Findings
- "75 Hard Together" (Google Play package `io.ionic.hard.together`, live since ~September 2024, ~1,500 installs per aggregator data) is built specifically around doing the challenge with friends: invite friends, track a team's combined progress, and "celebrate each other's achievements" — but one visible user review reports usability confusion ("So confused how to actually track the tasks? I started a challenge and there's no way to tick things off...") — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.ionic.hard.together&hl=en_US); [try.75hard.app](https://try.75hard.app/)
- "Forge 75 Hard & Soft Challenge" explicitly lets users "join a group, find your accountability partner, and finish 75 Hard together" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/ee/app/75-forge/id6746106520)
- "Daily Pact" supports creating a group with 75-Hard friends, separate daily challenges per task, check-ins, and a leaderboard showing overall completion rates across the group — [Daily Pact blog](https://daily-pact.com/blog/75-hard-with-friends)
- "Her 75" (a women-focused 75-day challenge app family, also branded "75 Her") lets users see friends' to-do lists and progress in real time, which its own marketing claims "no other app in this list does"; a related variant ("75 Her") adds a map feature showing friends' pins/locations with granular privacy control (share nothing, city-level, or exact location) — [her75.co](https://www.her75.co/); [75her.app](https://www.75her.app/)
- BeHard offers "friend invites with shared progress" as a feature alongside its AI coach and templates — [Reset75 comparison](https://reset75.com/compare/best-75-hard-apps/)
- Cited (secondhand) research claims accountability-partner behavior (weekly progress updates to a friend) raises goal completion from 43% to 76% — a large claimed effect, though the original study is not named/linked by the secondary source citing it — [Reset75 blog](https://reset75.com/blog/75-hard-accountability-partner/)
- A recognizable organic trend exists of people manually rigging shared tracking via their phone's default Notes app with friends (TikTok "75 Hard Habit Tracker on Your Notes App with Friends"), suggesting demand exceeds what dedicated apps currently satisfy well — [TikTok discover page](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/75-hard-habit-tracker-on-your-notes-app-with-friends)

### Inferences
- At least 5 distinct apps (75 Hard Together, Forge, Daily Pact, Her 75/75 Her, BeHard) now ship some form of group/friend visibility, which confirms the demand signal is real and being actively pursued by multiple small competitors — but install/download numbers found (only ~1,500 for 75 Hard Together) suggest none has achieved dominant scale or brand recognition yet, leaving room for a better-executed or differently-positioned (e.g., gamified) entrant.
- No competitor combines *group/shared tracking* with *game-like mechanics* (avatars, platformer, XP) — the group-tracking apps use leaderboards/real-time-status lists, not game worlds. Combining validated group demand with the confirmed-absent gamified/platformer angle (see section 2) appears to be genuine unclaimed territory.

### Gaps
- No independently verifiable download/revenue figures were found for most social-features apps beyond the single ~1,500-install data point for 75 Hard Together; App Store/Google Play do not publicly expose install counts for iOS, and Android figures found came from a third-party aggregator (AppBrain-style) rather than Google's own console, so precision is uncertain.
- The 43%→76% completion-rate statistic is repeated by at least one secondary source but its original study was not identified/linked — treat as an oft-cited but unverified figure pending a primary citation.
- Could not confirm review sentiment or churn specifically for the group-tracking features (e.g., do people actually keep using the friend-visibility feature past week 1?) — no longitudinal or retention data was found for any 75 Hard app.
